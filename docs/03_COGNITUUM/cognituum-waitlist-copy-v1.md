## The Pain (this is the section to hit hard)

### Continuity
You explained the architecture. You explained why. The model forgot by the
next session — and you're explaining it again, to the same model, about the
same decision, for the third time this month.

### Cost
Every re-explanation is a re-purchase of context you already paid for once.
Tokens spent re-establishing what should have simply persisted.

### Governance
Six months from now, someone asks why the system does what it does. The
answer lives in a chat log nobody's going to reread — not in a record anyone
can audit, trust, or hand off.

### Control — the one that actually hurts
The model doesn't ask before it overrides your call with something
"reasonable." It just does. You catch it in review, or you don't. Either
way, the decision that should have been yours got made by something that
doesn't have to live with the consequences.

---

## What changes

Cognituum is built on a simple inversion: **your engineering decisions
outrank the model's.** Once you decide something, that decision is captured,
versioned, and enforced — not re-litigated every time you open a new session
or switch providers. It's provider-agnostic by design: Claude, Codex,
Gemini, whatever you use, your judgment sits above all of them, not inside
one of them.

You stop repeating yourself. You just decide — and decide well, once.
